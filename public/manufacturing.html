FOOD, WEATHER, VISITS, AND A LOCAL DECISION

One small device. Several useful signals.

Keep the care loop useful even while the uplink is unavailable.

01

Measure what matters.

Ultrasonic headroom estimates the food reserve after empty / full calibration. A sheltered SHTC3 measures ambient temperature and humidity, away from processor heat and direct rain. Replaceable wetness probes flag a site check.

Food level is not food weight. Wetness is not a calibrated flood-depth or rainfall measurement. The selected sonar has a blind zone that constrains hopper geometry. Sensor specifications ↗

02

Remember locally.

Record a node identity, boot identity, sequence, timestamp, quality flags and optional visitor token. Buffer an event journal in flash with bounded retention; acknowledge packet identities at the hub before removing them.

Flash retention, clock recovery, storage wear and a durable device queue are proposed firmware work. The care-desk tiles simulate the sequence in the local app database.

03

Give people a next step.

Combine low food, a hot or cold period, and missing check-ins into an assigned inspection. A missing chip read prompts review of coverage and context; it cannot prove a cat is absent or unwell.

Use the same colony and caregiver workflow. Weather readings inform preparations; emergency access and welfare decisions belong to the care team.

This readable example is not an implemented radio protocol. Encode compactly for LoRa, batch routine sensing, and prioritize fault events. A boot identity plus sequence supports deduplication after reconnect. Keep raw chip IDs and caregiver contact details out of public packets; tokenization and link encryption still need implementation.

A VISIT IS AN OBSERVATION. A MEAL NEEDS MORE EVIDENCE.

A thoughtful feeding corner.

A no-contact reader loop beside an open approach; a covered mechanism above the bowl.

Download feeder figure ↓

The right reader for the chip.

Specify ISO 11784/11785 FDX-B support at 134.2 kHz and verify the actual cats’ chip formats. A 13.56 MHz RC522 / NFC reader is not compatible. Some legacy 125 / 128 kHz animal chips need a reader with explicit support.

Use a tuned, protected coil at neck height without enclosing the cat. Range varies with chip orientation, antenna size and nearby metal; do not promise a whole-bowl read zone. Pause the motor during a read window and validate interference.

Animal-chip guidance ↗ · Reader / antenna datasheet ↗

Useful visits, honest counts.

Group repeated reads from one chip into a visit after a quiet-gap rule. Count distinct caregiver-linked cats separately from raw reads and return visits. Unknown tokens wait for a caregiver association; no new cat identity is silently created.

An implanted chip supplies an identifier to a close reader. It does not locate a cat remotely or retrieve a verified name from a registry here. Keep unchipped cats in the same human-reviewed census.

A bounded gate, close to home.

A geared servo meters a short gate cycle with position feedback, a maximum run time, cooldown, and manual jam inspection. Guard all moving parts. A spring-biased closed design must be tested under load; an ordinary geared servo does not guarantee closure on power loss.

Requested grams are a calibration target. Position feedback cannot verify delivered weight. Test many cycles with the intended kibble, or add a separate calibrated load cell before claiming measured portions.

Feedback-servo reference ↗ · No chip read automatically dispenses food in this demo.

Power is part of the design.

Use protected 12 V input for the reference reader, a separately decoupled 5 V servo rail and clean 3.3 V logic. Select the regulator and supply against measured servo stall current plus radio / reader peaks. Verify level shifting, grounding, brownout behavior and gate state on reset. Solar, battery storage and runtime are outside this estimate.

FROM ILLUSTRATION TO A QUOTABLE PILOT

Build a little. Learn a lot.

The inexpensive board is one piece of a dependable outdoor station.

  1. 01 · Bench

    Verify chip formats, coil read zone, food sensor distance, servo torque and real power peaks with purchased modules.

  2. 02 · Design

    Create schematic, netlist and routed PCB. Review RF keep-outs, logic levels, thermal placement, test pads and assembly access.

  3. 03 · Quote

    Request BOM procurement, fabrication, stencil, placement, programming and test quotes. Confirm substitutions and regional radio requirements.

  4. 04 · Test

    Fixture-test rails, serial ports and probes. Calibrate the bin, verify jam / cooldown logic, deduplication and recovery after power loss.

  5. 05 · Pilot

    Run supervised stations with caregivers. Log missed reads, false levels, portion variation, maintenance time and site-specific link reliability.

What this page gives your team

Four editable SVG figures, an adjustable BOM, explicit budget assumptions and a downloadable design brief. The figures are illustrations, not Gerbers, a schematic, an electrical safety approval or a factory-ready PCB. The existing Arduino remains the working sensor demo.

For a nonprofit pilot, compare one sensing-only station with one upgraded station before buying a batch. Track refill response, manual reconciliation time and maintenance costs against a baseline; welfare benefits remain hypotheses until measured.
