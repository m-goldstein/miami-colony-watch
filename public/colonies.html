01 / THE COMMUNITY

The care is local.
The information is scattered.

The fictional Mangrove Mews brief describes 20 volunteers caring for 18 colonies, with identities, health concerns, and TNVR updates buried in chats. This prototype brings observations and visits into one shared record.

Our six demo colonies are fictional pilot data, not a directory of real feeding sites.

02 / THE CLIMATE

Notice the wetness.
Plan for the storm.

Miami-Dade identifies extreme heat, floods, and hurricanes as local hazards. A wet rain plate or water probe can prompt a station check; it does not replace official warnings or tell us the temperature.

03 / THE HUMAN RESPONSE

A signal needs
someone to show up.

Low food creates a refill task. Missing telemetry creates a connection check. Field notes link to individual cats, and confirmed TNVR needs a human evidence note. Hardware cannot diagnose an animal or count the colony.

The research & pilot plan

Primary sources, operating limits, community tradeoffs, and measurable outcomes.

Open full research ↗

What the device improves

  • Prioritize refill visits using calibrated hopper headspace.
  • Flag local wetness and protect supplies.
  • Keep useful measurements available at the station, even when its gateway is offline.
  • Track connection and maintenance work instead of treating missing data as safe.

What responsible deployment needs

  • Permission, suitable siting, and awareness of wildlife impacts.
  • Private site details, named backup caregivers, and storm plans.
  • Outdoor-rated protection, probe cleaning, and field calibration.
  • Human checks of food condition, cat identity, health, and TNVR evidence.
